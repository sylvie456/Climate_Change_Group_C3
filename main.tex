\title{Should the CiTru \COtwo{} storage pilot go ahead?}
\author{Sylvie Schläpfer (sschlaepfer)\\[0.5ex]
  \small Draft statement, case C3: \COtwo{} storage in Swiss aquifers\\
  \small Role play: expert of the Federal Office for the Environment (FOEN/BAFU)}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

The Climate and Innovation Act (KlG, Art.~3) binds Switzerland to net-zero
greenhouse gas emissions by 2050, by then permanent storage for about
12\,Mt \COtwo{} per year is expected to be needed
\cites{KlG}[p.~2]{FOEN2025Factsheet}{FOEN2025Legislation}.
The Project CiTru, which would inject about 5--25\,kt \COtwo{} into an aquifer
at about 1'100\,m depth at Trüllikon, with implementation planned for 2027
\cites{GruberBrunner2026}{4401_2026}, raises the question of whether such a
test should proceed before Swiss law has a storage regime.

FOEN supports CiTru only as a time-limited research project and asks the Canton
of Zurich, as licensing authority \cite[p.~6]{FOEN2025Factsheet}, to allow
injection only once its status under waste law is settled and the authorisation
requires an environmental assessment, independent monitoring with public data,
binding seismic stop thresholds and a named body responsible for closure,
aftercare and liability.

The Federal Council wants hard-to-avoid \COtwo{} stored
\foreignquote{ngerman}{nach Möglichkeit im Inland}, underground or in
long-lived products, and expects export to remain a challenge
\cite{Brupbacher2024}, but this aim cannot be tested or turned into sound law
without field data, since the national estimate of 2'680\,Mt is a first
appraisal that could not explicitly account for porosity, permeability, caprock
capillary entry pressure or salinity \cite[pp.~427, 452]{Chevalier2010}, and
the comparatively low permeability of the rock at Trüllikon is exactly what
this test would examine \cite{Brupbacher2024}.

Critics will object that \COtwo{} should not be injected before the law exists,
since Switzerland has no national legislation for underground storage, such
sites are not subject to an environmental impact assessment or the Major
Accidents Ordinance, waste law provides no type of site where \COtwo{} may be
deposited \cites[pp.~5--6]{FOEN2025Factsheet}[p.~109]{Affolter2024}, and
post-injection responsibility is a long-recognised weak point of storage
regulation \cite[pp.~255--257]{Benson2005}.
% TODO from Word draft: "(Erdbenben?)" -- mention earthquakes/induced seismicity here?

This objection is decisive for commercial storage, which must wait for the
framework act planned for 2031 \cite[pp.~6, 8]{FOEN2025Factsheet}, but not for
a test capped at 25\,kt, a quarter of the 100\,kt below which even the EU
excludes research storage from its CCS Directive
\cites[p.~13]{Affolter2024}{EU2009CCS}, provided a lawful basis is confirmed
first; pre-screening also puts simulated pressure changes on the nearby
Neuhausen Fault one to two orders of magnitude below reactivation levels and
sets a red-light stop at about $M_\mathrm{w}$~2.0
\cite[pp.~1, 11]{Schultz2024}, and swisstopo stresses that the test does not
decide whether or where Switzerland will store \COtwo{} \cite{Brupbacher2024}.

A strictly conditional, research-only CiTru therefore gives Switzerland the
evidence it needs for its storage decision without pre-empting that decision or
the law that must govern it.
